## Two imperfects run in parallel; imperfect + aorist means an interruption

Pairing [the imperfect](imperfect-formation.md) with [the aorist](aorist-past-tense.md) across a two-clause sentence signals two different real-world relationships, depending on which past tense each clause uses. Two imperfects together describe two actions happening at the same time, both ongoing: **Ενώ η Αρλέτα έψαχνε να παρκάρει, η Μελέκ μιλούσε με τον κύριο Αντρέα.** "While Arleta was looking for parking, Meleq was talking with Mr. Andrea" -- both simultaneously in progress.[^ellinika-b-ch7]

An imperfect paired with an aorist instead describes an ongoing action that a second, completed event interrupts or cuts across: **Την ώρα που πήγαινα στη στάση, πέρασε το λεωφορέιο.** "Just as I was heading to the stop, the bus went by (and I missed it)" -- the imperfect sets the ongoing backdrop, the aorist marks the single punctual event that happens within it.[^ellinika-b-ch7]

Both patterns are commonly introduced by the same small set of connectives -- **όταν, ενώ, την ώρα που, καθώς** -- so it's the choice of *tense* in each clause, not the connective itself, that signals which relationship is meant.[^ellinika-b-ch7]
