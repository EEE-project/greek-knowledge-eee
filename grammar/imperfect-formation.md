## The imperfect: built on the present stem, unlike the aorist

Where the [aorist](aorist-past-tense.md) is built from the simple-future stem, the imperfect (**παρατατικός**) -- Modern Greek's ongoing/habitual past ("I used to do," "I was doing") -- keeps the *present*-tense stem and just adds past endings and, where needed, the **ε-** augment: **παίζω → έπαιζα, έπαιζες, έπαιζε, παίζαμε, παίζατε, έπαιζαν**.[^ellinika-b-ch7] B-class verbs take a longer ending, **-ούσα**, built directly onto the present stem: **μπορώ → μπορούσα, μπορούσες, μπορούσε...**; some B1 verbs additionally allow a shorter colloquial alternative in **-αγα**: **περνάω → περνούσα** or **πέρναγα**, both current.[^ellinika-b-ch7]

A handful of common verbs have an irregular imperfect, with an **η-** augment instead of **ε-**: **θέλω → ήθελα**, **ξέρω → ήξερα**, **υπάρχω → υπήρχα**.[^ellinika-b-ch7]

The imperfect marks two related but distinguishable senses: **repetition** ("κάθε μέρα," every day/whenever) and **duration** ("όλη τη νύχτα," the whole night) -- both contrasted with the aorist's bounded, completed event.[^ellinika-b-ch7]
