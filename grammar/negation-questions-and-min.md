## Three negators with three different jobs

Modern Greek splits negation across three particles that don't substitute freely for one another. **Δεν** always precedes a finite indicative verb, negating a statement of fact: **Δεν κάνεις** "you don't do (it)," **Ο Νίκος δεν μένει εδώ** "Nikos doesn't live here."[^livexp-grammar] **Όχι** is the free-standing word "no" -- it answers a question on its own, or negates anything except a finite indicative verb directly: **Είσαι ο Κώστας; — Όχι.** "Are you Kostas? — No."[^livexp-grammar] **Μη(ν)** negates an imperative or a **να**-clause, never an ordinary indicative statement: **Δεν κάνεις** "you don't do it" contrasts with **Μην κάνεις!** "don't do it!"[^livexp-grammar]

- **Μην αγοράζεις!** -- "Don't buy (it)!"
- **Μην περιμένεις!** -- "Don't wait!"

Yes/no questions in Modern Greek are not marked by word order or a special particle; they're written with the Greek question mark (**;**, which looks like an English semicolon) and spoken with question intonation, with the sentence otherwise unchanged from a statement.[^livexp-grammar]
