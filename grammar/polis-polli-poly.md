## πολύς is irregular; πολύ the adverb never changes at all

**πολύς, πολλή, πολύ** "much, many" is an irregular adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender, number, and case like any other adjective (see [adjective-declension-and-suppletion](adjective-declension-and-suppletion.md) for the same kind of stem alternation in Ancient Greek's own μέγας/πολύς), but the **-λ-** doubles to **-λλ-** everywhere except the masculine and neuter singular (nominative and accusative). The textbook tabulates it -- singular nominative **πολύς, πολλή, πολύ**, accusative **πολύ, πολλή, πολύ**; plural nominative **πολλοί, πολλές, πολλά**, accusative **πολλούς, πολλές, πολλά** -- and shows it in use: **Υπήρχε πολύς κόσμος, πολλή δουλειά και πολύ στρες στο γραφείο.** "There were lots of people, lots of work and a lot of stress at the office.", **Με πολύ καφέ, πολλή ζάχαρη και πολύ γάλα.** "With lots of coffee, sugar and milk."[^ellinika-a]

The identically-spelled **πολύ** is also an indeclinable adverb, "very" or "a lot," placed before an adjective or another adverb, or after a verb: **Γνωρίζω έναν πολύ καλό παθολόγο.** "I know a very good internist.", **Σήμερα νιώθω πολύ καλά.** "Today I feel very well.", **Σήμερα βήχω πολύ!** "Today I'm coughing a lot!", **Φοβάμαι πολύ τους γιατρούς.** "I'm very afraid of doctors."[^ellinika-a] Only word position and whether it is agreeing with a noun tell the adjective and the adverb apart -- the adjective **πολύ** (neuter singular) can look identical to the adverb in a short phrase.
