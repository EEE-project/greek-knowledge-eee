## A noun's own ending hints at its related verb's conjugation

When a Modern Greek noun or adjective is morphologically related to a verb, the noun's ending correlates loosely with which present-tense conjugation (see [present-tense-b-verbs](present-tense-b-verbs.md) for the B-class pattern) that verb belongs to. Masculine **-ος** and neuter **-ο** nouns tend to pair with ordinary **-ω** verbs: **ο φίλος**, **το τηλέφωνο** → **τηλεφωνώ**. Masculine **-ης** and feminine **-η** nouns tend to pair with **-εις/-ει**-type verbs instead: **ο φοιτητής**, **η φίλη** → **σπουδάζω, σπουδάζεις, σπουδάζει**.[^ellinika-b-ch1]

This is a learnable tendency useful for guessing an unfamiliar verb's conjugation from a related, already-known noun -- not an exceptionless morphological rule.
