## Singular nominative/accusative endings by declension class

Modern Greek nouns fall into a handful of declension classes by their nominative singular ending, each with its own accusative singular ending: masculine **-ος → -ο** (**ο φίλος → τον φίλο**), masculine **-ας → -α** (**ο άντρας → τον άντρα**), masculine **-ης → -η** (**ο μαθητής → τον μαθητή**), and feminine **-α**/**-η** stay unchanged in the accusative (**η γυναίκα → τη γυναίκα**, **η φίλη → τη φίλη**) -- only the article marks the case there.[^ellinika-b-ch1] Neuter nouns (**-ο, -ι, -μα**) never distinguish nominative from accusative at all, in either number: **το βιβλίο**, **το παιδί**, **το μάθημα** stay identical as subject or object.[^ellinika-b-ch1]

A separate class of feminine nouns ends in **-ος** and declines exactly like the masculine **-ος** class despite its feminine gender -- **η οδός → την οδό**, plural **οι οδοί → τις οδούς**. This class covers a specific, learnable set: abstract/geographic nouns like **η είσοδος** "entrance," **η έξοδος** "exit," **η λεωφόρος** "avenue"; profession nouns that take either article depending on the referent's sex (**ο/η γιατρός, ο/η δικηγόρος, ο/η μηχανικός, ο/η οδηγός, ο/η πρόεδρος**); and a number of place names (**η Πάρος, η Ζάκυνθος, η Κόρινθος, η Αίγυπτος**).[^ellinika-b-ch1]
