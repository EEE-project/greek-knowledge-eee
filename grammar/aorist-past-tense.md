## The aorist: built from the simple-future stem, not the present

Modern Greek's aorist (**αόριστος**) marks a single, completed past event, in contrast to the imperfect's ongoing/habitual past. It is built from the same stem as the simple future -- once that stem is known (see [future-tha-aspect](future-tha-aspect.md)), the aorist adds personal endings **-α, -ες, -ε, -αμε, -ατε, -αν(ε)** to it, with stress pulled back three syllables from the end: **θα μιλήσω → μίλησα, μίλησες, μίλησε, μιλήσαμε, μιλήσατε, μίλησαν(ε)**.[^livexp-grammar]

When a stem has fewer than three syllables, a stressed **ε-** augment is added at the front in the singular and 3rd plural, but dropped in the 1st/2nd plural once the ending itself supplies enough syllables to carry the antepenultimate stress: **ζω → θα ζήσω → έζησα, έζησες, έζησε, ζήσαμε, ζήσατε, έζησαν** (not *εζήσαμε*).[^livexp-grammar]

A number of common, high-frequency verbs have an aorist built on a wholly different, suppletive stem rather than a regular one: **λέω → είπα**, **βλέπω → είδα**, **τρώω → έφαγα**, **πίνω → ήπια**, **φεύγω → έφυγα**, **βρίσκω → βρήκα**, **πάω → πήγα**, **παίρνω → πήρα**, **έρχομαι → ήρθα**.[^livexp-grammar]

Deponent/passive verbs in **-ομαι** form their aorist with **-στηκα** or **-θηκα** instead: **εργάζομαι → εργάστηκα**, **κοιμάμαι → κοιμήθηκα**, **σκέφτομαι → σκέφτηκα**, **θυμάμαι → θυμήθηκα**, **φοβάμαι → φοβήθηκα**.[^livexp-grammar]
