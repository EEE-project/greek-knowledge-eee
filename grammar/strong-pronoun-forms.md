## Strong pronouns: after a preposition, for contrast, or standing alone

Alongside the weak object clitics covered in [weak-pronouns-position](weak-pronouns-position.md) (**με, σε, τον, την, το...**), Modern Greek has a parallel set of strong, stressed personal pronoun forms (**εμένα, εσένα, αυτόν, αυτή(ν), αυτό, εμάς, εσάς, αυτούς, αυτές, αυτά**). The two sets aren't interchangeable: which one a sentence needs depends on the syntactic position, not on the speaker's choice of emphasis alone.[^ellinika-b-ch1]

A weak form can never follow a preposition -- only a strong form can: **από εμένα για εσένα** "from me to you," never *από με για σε*.[^ellinika-b-ch1] A strong form is also required as a one-word answer to a question (**– Ποιον περιμένεις; – Εσένα.**, never a bare *Σε*) and to mark contrast or emphasis, typically by fronting it before the verb -- **Εσένα ζήτησα** "It was you I asked for" (not Nikos).[^ellinika-b-ch1]

Fronting a strong form for emphasis does not remove the weak clitic the verb still needs: **Εμένα με φοβούνται όλοι** "Everyone is afraid of *me*" keeps **με** immediately before the verb even though **Εμένα** already leads the sentence.[^ellinika-b-ch1]
