## Case-determined meaning and the stylistic role of word order

Unlike many modern languages, Greek's core sentence meaning comes from case endings, not word order: **ὁ κλῆρός ἐστι μικρός** and **μικρός ἐστιν ὁ κλῆρος** mean exactly the same thing ("the plot is small"). Likewise **ὁ ἄνθρωπος γεωργεῖ τὸν κλῆρον** and **τὸν κλῆρον γεωργεῖ ὁ ἄνθρωπος** both mean "the man farms the plot" — the endings **-ος** (subject) and **-ον** (direct object) fix each word's role regardless of where it sits in the sentence.[^athenaze-ch1]

Word order still does real work, though — a stylistic and logical one. The word a speaker wants to emphasize is usually moved to the front of the sentence: putting the direct object first, as in **τὸν κλῆρον γεωργεῖ ὁ ἄνθρωπος**, throws emphasis onto *the plot* specifically — "it's the *plot* (and not something else) that the man farms".[^athenaze-ch1]
