## A third adjective pattern: -ύς / -ιά / -ύ

Beyond the two 1st/2nd-declension adjective shapes already covered in [adjective-declension-and-suppletion](adjective-declension-and-suppletion.md) for Ancient Greek, Modern Greek keeps a distinct third pattern surviving from a different ancient class: **-ύς** (masc.), **-ιά** (fem.), **-ύ** (neut.) -- **φαρδύς, φαρδιά, φαρδύ** "wide, loose-fitting." The masculine nominative plural is **-οι** on the feminine-looking stem (**οι φαρδιοί**, not *οι φαρδύοι*), and the masculine genitive singular has two current variants, **του φαρδιού** alongside a less common **του φαρδύ**.[^ellinika-b-ch4]

A fixed set of common adjectives shares this exact pattern: **μακρύς** "long," **βαρύς** "heavy," **βαθύς** "deep," **πλατύς** "broad," **ελαφρύς** "light," **παχύς** "fat, thick."[^ellinika-b-ch4]

- **Στο σημείο αυτό το ποτάμι γίνεται πολύ βαθύ.** "At this point the river gets very deep."
- **Όταν ήταν νέος, ο Άγγελος είχε μακριά σγουρά μαλλιά!** "When he was young, Angelos had long curly hair!"
