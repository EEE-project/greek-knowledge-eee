## An article alone turns an adjective into a noun

Any Modern Greek adjective can function as a noun simply by taking an article with no noun following it; the article and adjective still agree in gender, number, and case, and the missing noun is supplied by context ("the [married] one," "the [good] thing").[^livexp-grammar]

- **παντρεμένος** "married" → **ο παντρεμένος** "the married man"
- **τρελός** "crazy" → **ο τρελός** "the crazy one / a madman"
- **άτακτος** "naughty" → **ο άτακτος** / **η άτακτη** "the naughty boy / girl"
- **κακό** "bad" → **το κακό** "the bad thing, evil"

The construction is fully productive, but the resulting word's exact idiomatic meaning -- often narrower or more specific than a literal "the [adjective] one" -- needs to be checked per adjective rather than assumed.[^livexp-grammar]
