## κανένας/τίποτα: the same word means "any" or "none" depending on the sentence

**κανένας/κανείς** (masc.), **καμία/καμιά** (fem.), **κανένα** (neut.), and **τίποτα/τίποτε** (indeclinable, "anything/nothing") do double duty. In a question or an affirmative context they mean "any(one)/some(thing)," equivalent to **κάποιος/κάτι**: **Είναι κανείς εδώ;** "Is anyone here?", **Θέλεις τίποτα από το περίπτερο;** "Do you want anything from the kiosk?"[^ellinika-b-ch3] In a negative clause (with **δεν/δε**) the very same words instead mean "no one/nothing at all": **Κανένας δεν ήταν στη στάση.** "No one was at the stop," **Δε θέλω τίποτα.** "I don't want anything (at all)."[^ellinika-b-ch3]

Which reading applies is determined by the sentence's polarity, not by the word itself -- the same lexical item, opposite English translations.[^ellinika-b-ch3]
