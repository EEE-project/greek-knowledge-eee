## λέω itself shifts meaning by which clause follows it

**λέω** "to say" changes its own sense depending on whether it governs a **να**-clause or an **ότι/πως**-clause. **Λέω να** + subjunctive marks something only being considered, not yet decided: **Λέει να πάει με τα πόδια.** "He's thinking of walking" (not sure yet).[^ellinika-b-ch12] **Λέω ότι/πως** + indicative instead states a fact the speaker is asserting with confidence: **Λέει ότι θα πάει με τα πόδια.** "He says he's walking" (he's decided, stating it as fact).[^ellinika-b-ch12]

The same surface verb, **λέω**, therefore signals two different speech acts -- tentatively floating an idea versus flatly asserting one -- purely through which clause type follows it, the same way [subjunctive-triggers-and-ambiguity](subjunctive-triggers-and-ambiguity.md) already shows **μπορεί** shifting sense by context alone.
