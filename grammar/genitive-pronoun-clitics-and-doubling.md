## Genitive clitics follow the same weak/strong split as the accusative

The weak genitive clitics (**μου, σου, του, της, του, μας, σας, τους**) mark an indirect object and precede the verb, exactly like the accusative weak forms covered in [weak-pronouns-position](weak-pronouns-position.md): **Μου έδωσε το τηλέφωνό του.** "He gave me his phone number."[^ellinika-b-ch2] They have their own strong counterparts too (**εμένα, εσένα, αυτού/αυτουνού, αυτής/αυτηνής, εμάς, εσάς, αυτών**), required in the same contrastive/emphatic contexts as [strong-pronoun-forms](strong-pronoun-forms.md) describes for the accusative -- fronting the strong form for contrast never removes the weak clitic the verb still needs: **Εμένα μου έδωσε το τηλέφωνό του. Εσένα;** "He gave the number to *me*. You?"[^ellinika-b-ch2]

A genitive and an accusative weak clitic can stack together before the same verb -- indirect object first, then direct object: **Μου το έδωσε.** "He gave it to me." The final vowel of **μου/σου/του** elides before the following **το**, giving colloquial contractions like **μού 'δωσε το** written as **μου το 'δωσε**, or imperative **Δώσ' μου το**.[^ellinika-b-ch2]
