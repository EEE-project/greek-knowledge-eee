## αν και / ενώ: conceding a fact that doesn't change the outcome

**Αν και** ("although"), and **ενώ** used the same way, introduces a concessive clause -- a fact that would normally point toward a different outcome, set against a main clause that happens anyway: **Αν και η Σαντορίνη έχει πολύ κόσμο αυτή την εποχή, περνάμε υπέροχα.** "Although Santorini is crowded this time of year, we're having a wonderful time."[^ellinika-b-ch11]

**ενώ** doing concessive duty here is a second job for a word Modern Greek also uses to mean simultaneous "while" (see [combining-imperfect-aorist](combining-imperfect-aorist.md)) -- context, not the word itself, signals which relationship is meant: **Αν και / Ενώ δεν είχαν πολλά χρήματα μαζί τους, πέρασαν πολύ καλά στις διακοπές.** "Even though they didn't have much money with them, they had a great time on vacation."[^ellinika-b-ch11]
