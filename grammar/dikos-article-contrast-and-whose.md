## The article turns δικός μου from "mine" into "MINE, not yours"

Building on [possessives-and-dikos](possessives-and-dikos.md)'s **δικός + possessive pronoun** construction: whether the phrase carries its own definite article changes its force. Without the article, **δικός μου** is a plain statement of ownership -- **Αυτό το σπίτι είναι δικό μου.** "This house is mine (I'm the owner)." With the article, it becomes contrastive -- **Αυτό το σπίτι είναι το δικό μου.** "This house is *mine* (not the other one)."[^ellinika-b-ch2]

**οι δικοί μου** as a fixed idiom means "my family/people," not literally "my own [things]": **Χαιρετίσματα στους δικούς σου.** "Regards to your family."[^ellinika-b-ch2]

To ask whose something is, Modern Greek has several interchangeable question words -- **Ποιανού/ποιανής είναι;**, **Ποιου/ποιας είναι;**, and the indeclinable **Τίνος είναι;** (same form for any gender) -- all answered with a bare **δικός/δική/δικό + pronoun**, never a bare weak possessive alone: **– Τίνος είναι το αυτοκίνητο; – Δικό μου είναι.**[^ellinika-b-ch2]
