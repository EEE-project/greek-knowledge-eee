## What is pleasing agrees with αρέσει, not the person who likes it

**αρέσω** "to please, be pleasing" expresses liking the reverse way round from English "to like": the person who likes something is a weak genitive clitic (**μου, σου, του, της, μας, σας, τους**), while the thing liked is the grammatical subject and stays in the nominative. Because the liked thing is nearly always 3rd person, the verb is used almost exclusively in the 3rd person, agreeing in number with that subject: singular **αρέσει** for one thing liked, plural **αρέσουν** for more than one.[^livexp-grammar]

- **Μου αρέσει το δάσος.** -- "I like the forest" (literally "the forest is pleasing to me").
- **Μου αρέσουν οι γάτες.** -- "I like cats."
- **Μ'αρέσουν οι γυναίκες, αλλά σ'αρέσουν οι άντρες.** -- "I like women, but you like men."

The liked thing must stay nominative even when it's easy to mistake for a direct object: **Μου αρέσει ο σκύλος**, never an accusative **τον σκύλο**.[^livexp-grammar]

To express liking an activity rather than a thing, **αρέσει** governs a **να**-clause in the present -- always the ongoing/habitual present, never a one-off event, since "liking to do something" is inherently a standing preference: **Μου αρέσει να κολυμπάω** "I like swimming."[^livexp-grammar]

Two related impersonal constructions share the same genitive-clitic-plus-nominative-subject shape as **αρέσει** but cover a narrower, more concrete sense each. **μου πάει** "it suits me" judges how something looks on the wearer: **Σου πάει!** "It suits you!" **μου κάνει** "it fits/works for me" judges practical fit: **Δε μου κάνει.** "It doesn't fit me."[^ellinika-a]
