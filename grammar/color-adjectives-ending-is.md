## Color adjectives: -ής/-ιά/-ί, often left uninflected in practice

A distinct adjective pattern, **-ής** (masc.), **-ιά** (fem.), **-ί** (neut.), covers a group of Modern Greek color terms borrowed from a noun for the colored substance: **καφετής, καφετιά, καφετί** "brown" (from **καφές** "coffee"), and by the same pattern **πορτοκαλί** "orange" (from **πορτοκάλι**), **σοκολατί** "chocolate-brown," **σταχτί** "ash-grey," **χρυσαφί** "golden."[^ellinika-b-ch4]

- **Τίνος είναι αυτός ο καφετής σκύλος;** "Whose brown dog is this?"
- **Εκείνο το πορτοκαλί κασκόλ μού αρέσει πολύ.** "I really like that orange scarf."

In casual, everyday usage these color adjectives are very often left in their indeclinable neuter-singular form regardless of the noun's actual gender or number, rather than fully inflected: **τα καφετιά παπούτσια** or, just as commonly, **τα καφετί παπούτσια** "the brown shoes" -- both are heard, with no difference in meaning.[^ellinika-b-ch4]
