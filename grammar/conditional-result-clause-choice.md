## The "then" half of a real conditional need not be a plain future

[conditional-type-1](conditional-type-1.md) already covers the **αν**-clause itself (bare future stem, no **θα**); its result clause turns out to have two valid forms, not just the ordinary **θα**-future one. Alongside **Αν έρθετε στην Ελλάδα τον Ιούνιο, θα βρείτε κάτι καλό.** "If you come to Greece in June, you'll find something good," the result clause can instead be a plain [subjunctive](subjunctive-triggers-and-ambiguity.md) with no **θα** at all, typically for an offer or invitation rather than a prediction: **Αν έρθετε στο νησί, να σας φιλοξενήσουμε.** "If you come to the island, let us host you."[^ellinika-b-ch11]

The **θα**-future result states what will simply happen; the subjunctive result instead offers, invites, or suggests what the speaker is willing to do -- the same underlying if-clause, two different pragmatic colors depending on which mood follows.[^ellinika-b-ch11]
