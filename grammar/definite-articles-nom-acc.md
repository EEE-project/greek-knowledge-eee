## The definite article: nominative and accusative

The Modern Greek definite article agrees with its noun in gender, number, and case: **ο/η/το** (nominative singular), **τον/την/το** (accusative singular), **οι/οι/τα** (nominative plural), **τους/τις/τα** (accusative plural).[^livexp-grammar]

Neuter nouns keep the identical article and noun form across nominative and accusative, singular and plural alike (**το σπίτι** as subject or object, **τα σπίτια** either way) — subject and object can only be told apart by word order or context, not by the article.[^livexp-grammar] For masculine and feminine nouns, only the article marks the case distinction directly: **Ο Κώστας βλέπει την Άννα** ("Kostas sees Anna") -- the noun endings themselves often don't change (proper names especially), so the article carries the grammatical weight.[^livexp-grammar]

The final **-ν** of the masculine **τον** is always written (**ρωτάω τον διευθυντή**), which also keeps it apart from neuter **το**; the feminine **την** keeps its **-ν** only before a vowel or **κ, π, τ, ξ, ψ, γκ, μπ, ντ** and drops it before any other consonant (**στην Αρλέτα**, but **για τη δουλειά**).[^wikipedia-teliko-ni][^ellinika-a] The same rule governs **δεν/μην**; for **έναν** and the feminine **μια(ν)** see [indefinite-article-enas](indefinite-article-enas.md).
