## A closed class of nouns gains a whole extra syllable in the plural

Alongside the regular plural patterns in [masculine-feminine-noun-plurals](masculine-feminine-noun-plurals.md), a specific, listable set of Greek nouns -- mostly informal/colloquial family terms and -tzis-type occupation nouns -- forms its plural by inserting **-δ-** before the ordinary ending, adding a full extra syllable: **ο μπαμπάς → οι μπαμπάδες** "dads," **ο καφές → οι καφέδες** "coffees," **ο μανάβης → οι μανάβηδες** "greengrocers," **ο καφετζής → οι καφετζήδες** "coffee-shop owners," **η μαμά → οι μαμάδες** "moms."[^ellinika-b-ch6] The genitive plural takes the same extra syllable: **των μπαμπάδων, των καφέδων**.[^ellinika-b-ch6]

Occupation nouns ending in **-τζής** (from Turkish **-cı**, "one who does/deals in X") follow this same **-ήδες** pattern: **ταξιτζής** "taxi driver," **μπογιατζής** "painter," **σουβλατζής** "souvlaki-seller."[^ellinika-b-ch6] **ο παππούς** "grandfather" (→ **οι παππούδες**) is the only common noun following the **-ούς → -ούδες** pattern.[^ellinika-b-ch6]

This is a closed, memorizable list rather than a productive rule applying to every noun with a matching ending -- most masculine **-ας/-ης** nouns still take the ordinary **-ες** plural described in [masculine-feminine-noun-plurals](masculine-feminine-noun-plurals.md).
