## Present middle indicative

The middle voice uses its own set of personal endings, distinct from the active: **-ομαι, -ῃ, -εται, -όμεθα, -εσθε, -ονται**. Built on λύω "loose, free": **λύομαι**, **λύῃ**, **λύεται**, **λυόμεθα**, **λύεσθε**, **λύονται**.[^smyth-104]

In the present and imperfect systems these endings are shared between middle and passive — **λύομαι** can mean either "I release for myself" (middle) or "I am released" (passive); only context tells them apart. In the future and aorist, though, the passive uses its own distinct -θη-/-η- stem, separate from the middle's forms.[^smyth-104]

The middle voice broadly presents the subject as specially involved in, benefiting from, or affected by the action, rather than narrowly meaning "acts on itself."[^smyth-104] Two recurring patterns are worth naming for a beginner: the direct/reflexive middle (the subject acts on itself) and the indirect or "dynamic" middle, where the subject acts for its own interest or with its own resources — a father who **λύεται** his son from captivity ransoms him with his own money, as opposed to the plain active **λύει**, "he sets him free."[^lsj-luo] A separate, large group of verbs are **deponent**: middle or passive in form but active in meaning, many with no active counterpart in ordinary use — **ἔρχομαι** "I come/go," **γίγνομαι** "I become," **δέχομαι** "I receive," and **μάχομαι** "I fight" are common examples a beginner meets early.[^smyth-104]
