## B-class verbs: a "contracted" and a "full" conjugation, often interchangeable

Modern Greek's B-class (contract) present-tense verbs split into two conjugation patterns that differ only in form, not in meaning or use. Group B1 verbs end **-άω** (stress on the **ά**): **μιλάω, μιλάς, μιλάει, μιλάμε, μιλάτε, μιλάνε** ("to speak"). Group B2 verbs end **-ώ** (stress on the ending itself): **τηλεφωνώ, τηλεφωνείς, τηλεφωνεί, τηλεφωνούμε, τηλεφωνείτε, τηλεφωνούν** ("to phone").[^livexp-grammar]

Many individual verbs exist in both a B1 and a near-equivalent B2 citation form (**μιλάω / μιλώ**, **αγαπάω / αγαπώ**) with no difference in meaning between them; ongoing language change is slowly favoring B1 forms for verbs that historically had a B2 form, so both are heard side by side.[^livexp-grammar]

A small, closed set of common verbs -- **πάω, λέω, ακούω, τρώω, κλαίω, φταίω** -- don't end in **-άω** or **-ώ** at all but conjugate like B1 anyway (group "AB"): **πάω, πας, πάει, πάμε, πάτε, πάνε**; **λέω, λες, λέει, λέμε, λέτε, λένε**.[^livexp-grammar]
