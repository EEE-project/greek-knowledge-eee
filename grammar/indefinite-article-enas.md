## The indefinite article: forms of ένας

Modern Greek's indefinite article is an inflected form of the numeral **ένας** "one" -- introducing one non-specific countable noun, agreeing with it in gender and case: **ένας φίλος**, **μία γυναίκα**, **ένα βιβλίο**.[^livexp-grammar]

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominative | ένας | μία | ένα |
| Accusative | έναν | μία | ένα |
| Genitive | ενός | μίας | ενός |

The textbook sets the three accusatives side by side: **Θέλω έναν χυμό.**, **Θέλω μία σοκολάτα.**, **Θέλω ένα παγωτό.** The feminine appears both as **μία** and as **μια**, with a bracketed **-ν** in the accusative entry of its table (**μια(ν)**);[^ellinika-a] the LiveXP table brackets the masculine accusative instead (**ένα(ν)**).[^livexp-grammar] Standard written Greek treats the two genders differently: the final **-ν** of a masculine form (**τον, έναν, αυτόν**) is always written, which also keeps it apart from the neuter (**το, ένα, αυτό**), whereas the feminine **την** and the particles **δεν/μην** keep theirs only before a vowel or **κ, π, τ, ξ, ψ, γκ, μπ, ντ** and drop it before any other consonant (**τη δασκάλα**, **δε θέλω**).[^wikipedia-teliko-ni]

The tables give the indefinite article in the singular only: a plural noun simply stands bare, as in the textbook's shopping phrase **Δύο χυμούς, τρεις σοκολάτες και τέσσερα παγωτά.**[^ellinika-a]
