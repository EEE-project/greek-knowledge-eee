## The lost infinitive: να + subjunctive as one of its replacements

Ancient Greek had a richly differentiated infinitive paradigm — traditionally counted as thirteen distinct forms across tense/aspect and voice — covering uses from purpose and complementation to accusative-and-infinitive constructions. It declined gradually from the post-classical period onward and had disappeared as a productive category by the late medieval period, unevenly: different infinitival constructions were replaced at different times, not all at once.[^osu-loss-of-infinitive] In many of the environments where Ancient Greek used an infinitive, Modern Greek instead uses the particle **να** (ultimately from ἵνα) followed by a subjunctive verb, with its own person and number set by the subject of that clause -- not simply a new infinitive, but a finite clause that took over many, though not all, of the infinitive's old functions, while also developing hortative and desiderative uses the infinitive itself never had.[^osu-loss-of-infinitive]
