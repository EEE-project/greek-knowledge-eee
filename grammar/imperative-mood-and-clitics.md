## The imperative: two endings, a negative built on the subjunctive, and a clitic that changes sides

The imperative (**προστακτική**) has one form for addressing one person and another for several: singular **-ε**, plural **-τε** -- **άνοιξε/ανοίξτε**, **γράψε/γράψτε**, **δώσε/δώστε**, **πάρε/πάρτε**.[^ellinika-a] A small group of very common verbs has short irregular forms in **-ες/-είτε** instead: **βλέπω → δες/δείτε**, **λέω → πες/πείτε**, **βρίσκω → βρες/βρείτε**, **πίνω → πιες/πιείτε**, **βγαίνω → βγες/βγείτε**, **μπαίνω → μπες/μπείτε**; **έρχομαι** has **έλα/ελάτε**.[^ellinika-a]

A weak pronoun *follows* an affirmative imperative, the reverse of its usual place in front of the verb (see [weak-pronouns-position](weak-pronouns-position.md)):[^livexp-grammar] **Κοίταξέ με.**, **Άνοιξέ τον.**, **Διάβασέ την.**, **Τηλεφώνησέ μου.**[^ellinika-a] The clitic also affects the accent. An imperative stressed on the third syllable from the end (**κοίταξε, άνοιξε, διάβασε**) takes a second accent on its last syllable once the clitic is attached -- the same second-stress behaviour a noun shows before a possessive clitic (see [possessives-and-dikos](possessives-and-dikos.md)) -- while one stressed on the second-to-last syllable (**κλείσε, γράψε, στείλε**) is commonly written with its final **-ε** elided instead: **Κλείσ' τον.**, **Γράψ' τες.**, **Στείλ' το.**[^ellinika-a]

A negative command is not a negated imperative: it is **μη(ν)** plus the subjunctive form, i.e. the **να**-clause with the **να** dropped -- the textbook sets **να μη διαβάσεις** equal to **μη διαβάσεις**, just as it sets **να διαβάσεις** equal to **διάβασε**.[^ellinika-a] The weak pronoun goes back in front of the verb here: **Περίμενέ με.** but **Μη με περιμένεις.**; **Τηλεφώνησέ μου.** but **Μη μου τηλεφωνήσεις.**[^ellinika-a] The verb after **μη(ν)** can also be in the present-tense form, as in **Μην κάνεις**, **Μην έρχεσαι**, **Μην περιμένεις**[^livexp-grammar] and the textbook's **μη μου γράφετε**.[^ellinika-a]
