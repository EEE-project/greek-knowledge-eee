## Cause, purpose, and reported content: three ways to hang a second clause on a sentence

**Cause.** A **Γιατί;** question is answered with **επειδή** and a full clause: **– Γιατί έχεις άγχος; – Επειδή αύριο έχω συνέντευξη.** "– Why are you stressed? – Because I have an interview tomorrow."[^ellinika-a] **γιατί** also joins two clauses mid-sentence in the sense "because," as in an exercise sentence of Unit 18, **Σήμερα άργησα να φτάσω στη δουλειά, γιατί είχε ... κίνηση στον δρόμο.**[^ellinika-a]

**Purpose.** **για να** plus the verb in its **να**-form says what an action is done for: **Πήρα τηλέφωνο για να ρωτήσω για τη δουλειά.** "I phoned to ask about the job."[^ellinika-a] The verb after **για να** is the same dependent form that follows **να** on its own (see [na-dependent-verb-forms](na-dependent-verb-forms.md)).

**Reported content.** After verbs of perceiving, deciding, and thinking, the content is introduced by **ότι** and keeps its own ordinary verb: **Όταν πήγα στο ταμείο, κατάλαβα ότι δεν είχα το πορτοφόλι μου.** "When I went to the cash desk, I realised I didn't have my wallet.", **Αποφασίσαμε ότι είστε το άτομο που χρειαζόμαστε.** "We decided that you are the person we need."[^ellinika-a] **πως** appears in the same position in the textbook's dialogues (**Νομίζω πως κάτι ετοιμάζουν ...**).[^ellinika-a] For how **λέω ότι** differs from **λέω να**, see [leei-na-vs-leei-oti](leei-na-vs-leei-oti.md); for **αν**-clauses, see [conditional-type-1](conditional-type-1.md).
