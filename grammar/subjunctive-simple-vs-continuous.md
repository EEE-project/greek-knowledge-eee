## The subjunctive splits into simple/continuous, the same way the future does

The [simple-vs-continuous aspect split](continuous-future-signal-words.md) already familiar from **θα** carries over to **να**-clauses unchanged: a simple subjunctive (aorist stem) marks a single, bounded action, a continuous subjunctive (present stem) marks an ongoing or repeated one -- **Μπορείς να φας ό,τι θέλεις σήμερα.** "You can eat whatever you like today (this once)" against **Μπορείς να τρως ό,τι θέλεις από αύριο.** "You can eat whatever you like from tomorrow on (as an ongoing rule)."[^ellinika-b-ch12] The same split extends to impersonal expressions of possibility/permission: **επιτρέπεται/απαγορεύεται/είναι δυνατόν να**, and to **το να** + subjunctive used as a clause's own grammatical subject.[^ellinika-b-ch12]

Certain verbs and expressions govern almost exclusively one aspect rather than genuinely allowing both. Verbs of beginning/continuing/stopping/habituation take the continuous subjunctive nearly every time, since starting or stopping *a repeated action* is inherently about an ongoing pattern, not a single event: **Άρχισα να καπνίζω.** "I started smoking," **Σταμάτησα να καπνίζω.** "I stopped smoking," **Συνηθίζω να οδηγώ προσεκτικά.** "I usually drive carefully."[^ellinika-b-ch12] Conversely, expressions of a single completed or imminent action take the simple subjunctive almost exclusively: **Πάω να αγοράσω ψωμί.** "I'm off to buy bread," **Ξέχασα να αγοράσω εφημερίδα.** "I forgot to buy a newspaper," **Δεν πρόλαβα να διαβάσω.** "I didn't get a chance to read."[^ellinika-b-ch12]

**να** also introduces deliberative questions -- asking what one *should* do, not what *is* the case: **Τι να κάνουμε απόψε;** "What should we do tonight?", **Πού να πάμε;** "Where should we go?"[^ellinika-b-ch12]
