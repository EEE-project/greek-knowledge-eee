## Ancient Greek meaning

κουρο-τρόφος, ον, rearing children, rare in lit. sense, **[1st c. AD]** γυνὴ νεοτόκος καὶ κ. Aret. CA 2.3: usu. metaph., ἀγαθὴ κ. good nursing-mother, of Ithaca, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 9.27, cf. **[5th c. BC]** Pi. Fr. 109; **[5th c. BC]** κ. Ἑλλάς E. Tr. 566(lyr., s.v.l.); Ἀπόλλωνος κ., of Delos, **[3rd c. BC]** Call. Del. 2, **[3rd c. BC]** 276: freq. as epith. of goddesses, as Hecate, **[7th c. BC]** Hes. Th. 450; **[1st c. BC]** Ἄρτεμις D.S. 5.73;Λοχία Supp.Epigr. 3.400.9(Delph., iii B. C.); of the Roman goddess Rumina, **[1st/2nd c. AD]** Plu. 2.278d; esp. of Aphrodite, **[8th c. BC]** Hom. Epigr. 12; called ἡ K. alone,IG 12.840.9, **[5th c. BC]** Ar. Th. 299, **[5th/4th c. BC]** Pl.Com.174.7, **[2nd c. AD]** Luc. DMeretr. 5.1; αἱ πύλαι αἱ κατὰ Κουροτρόφον, at Delos,IG 11(2).203A 46(iii B. C.):—in form Κουροτρόπος, ὁ (sc. μήν), name of Acarnanian month, ib.5(1).29.11(Sparta).[^lsj]
