## Ancient Greek meaning

 ἀσκηθής, ές, unhurt, unscathed, in Hom. of persons, **[8th c. BC]** ἂψ εἰς ἡμέας ἔλθοι ἀ. Il. 10.212; **[8th c. BC]** ἀ. ἱκόμην ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν Od. 9.79, cf. **[5th c. BC]** Epich. 99.10, **[3rd c. BC]** Call. Aet. 3.1.69; ἀσκηθέες (trisyll.) **[8th c. BC]** καὶ ἄνουσοι Od. 14.255 ( v.l. ἀσκεθέες ); sound, healthy, IG 4.952.109 (Epid.); **[6th c. BC]** ἀ. τινὰ πέμπειν Sol. 19.4; unblemished, IG 5(2).3.5 (Tegea, iv B. C.); = ἀπαθής, θεός Timo 60, etc.; later of things, ἀ. νόστος safe return, **[3rd c. BC]** A.R. 2.690; ἀ. μέλι pure, virgin honey, **[5th/4th c. BC]** Antim. 16.2. (Perh. from ἀ- priv., and the root of scathe, **[1st c. BC/AD]** Germ. schaden 'hurt'.) [^lsj]
