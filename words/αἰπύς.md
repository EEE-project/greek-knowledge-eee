## Ancient Greek meaning

 αἰπύς, εῖα, ύ, Ep. and Lyr. Adj., rare in Trag., high and steep, in Hom. mostly of cities on rocky heights, esp. of Troy, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 3.485, al.; of hills, **[8th c. BC]** Il. 2.603; later of the sky, **[5th c. BC]** αἰθήρ B. 3.36; **[5th c. BC]** οὐρανός S. Aj. 845; on high, **[7th c. BC]** ποδῶν αἰ. ἰωή Hes. Th. 682; ἁψαμένη βρόχον αἰπύν hanging high, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 11.278. metaph., sheer, utter, αἰ. ὄλεθρος freq. in Hom., death being regarded as the plunge from a high precipice; φόνος αἰ. **[8th c. BC]** Od. 4.843; **[5th c. BC]** θάνατος Pi. O. 10(11).42; σκότος utter darkness, **[5th c. BC]** Id. Fr. 228; of passions, etc., αἰ. χόλος towering wrath, **[8th c. BC]** Il. 15.223; δόλος αἰ. **[7th c. BC]** h.Merc. 66, **[7th c. BC]** Hes. Th. 589; αἰπυτάτη σοφίη AP 11.354 ( **[6th c. AD]** Agath.); arduous, **[8th c. BC]** πόνος Il. 11.601, **[8th c. BC]** 16.651; αἰπύ οἱ ἐσσεῖται 'twill be hard work for him, **[8th c. BC]** 13.317.
