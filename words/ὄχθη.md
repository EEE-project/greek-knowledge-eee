## Ancient Greek meaning

ὄχθη, ἡ, older form of ὄχθος, any height or rising ground, natural or artificial, bank, dyke by the side of a river, **[8th c. BC]** ὑψηλὴν βάλεν ὄχθην Il. 21.171, cf. **[8th c. BC]** 172: in sg., also, **[1st c. AD]** Plu. Publ. 16, **[2nd c. AD]** Arr. An. 1.14.4,CPHerm. 95.10(iii A. D.): mostly in pl., raised banks of a river, in full, **[8th c. BC]** ποταμοῖο παρ' ὄχθας Il. 4.487, **[8th c. BC]** 18.533, cf. **[8th c. BC]** 3.187; **[8th c. BC]** παρ' ὄχθῃσιν ποταμοῖο Od. 6.97; **[5th c. BC]** Καφισοῦ παρ' ὄχθαις Pi. P. 4.46, cf. **[6th c. BC]** Xenoph. 2.21, **[5th c. BC]** A. Pr. 810, **[5th c. BC]** Th. 392, etc.; ὄχθαι καπέτοιο the raised banks of the trench, dykes, **[8th c. BC]** Il. 15.356; also, heights beside the sea, **[8th c. BC]** ἁλὸς παρ' ὄχθας Od. 9.132; ταὶ ὑπὲρ Κύμας ἁλιερκέες ὄ. **[5th c. BC]** Pi. P. 1.18, cf. **[5th c. BC]** 12.2; also of rising banks at a little distance from a river, **[4th c. BC]** X. An. 4.3.3and **[4th c. BC]** 5: ὄχθη is distd. as the bank of a river, from ὄχθος a hill, in **[5th c. BC]** S. Ph. 726, **[5th c. BC]** 729(both lyr.); and this distn. generally holds, but in **[5th c. BC]** Pi. P. 1.64we read ὄχθαις ὑπὸ Ταϋγέτου; and in **[5th c. BC]** S. Ant. 1132(lyr.), Νυσαίων ὀρέων ὄχθαι; reversely, we have in Sapph. **[5th c. BC]** p.44Lobel, ὄχθοις Ἀχέροντος; in **[5th c. BC]** A. Ag. 1161, Ἀχερουσίους ὄχθους; and in **[5th c. BC]** E. Supp. 655, Ἰσμήνιον πρὸς ὄχθον; in late Prose, τὴν[^lsj]
