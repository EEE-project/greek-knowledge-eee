## Ancient Greek meaning

 ὑπερφία^λος [ι^], ον, overbearing, overweening, arrogant, of persons, freq. in Homer, in Il. of the Trojans, **[8th c. BC]** 13.621, **[8th c. BC]** 21.459, al.; in Od. of the Cyclopes, **[8th c. BC]** 9.106 (of the Cyclopes in good sense, **[5th c. BC]** B. 10.78); more freq. of the suitors, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 1.134, **[8th c. BC]** 2.310, al.; **[5th c. BC]** Γίγαντες B. 14.62; ὑ. γόνος, of a Centaur, **[5th c. BC]** Pi. P. 2.42, cf. **[5th c. BC]** O. 10(11).34, **[5th c. BC]** P. 4.111; also θυμὸς ὑ. an arrogant spirit, **[8th c. BC]** Il. 15.94; ἔπος, μῦθοι ὑ., **[8th c. BC]** Od. 4.503, **[8th c. BC]** 774.—Orig. the word seems only to have signified puissant, without any bad sense, as is prob. from **[8th c. BC]** Od. 21.289, where Antinous uses it of himself and the rest of the suitors, ὑπερφιάλοισι μεθ' ἡμῖν δαίνυσαι; and **[3rd/2nd c. BC]** Aristarch. read ὑπερφίαλον for ὑπέρθυμον in **[8th c. BC]** Il. 5.881: later writers also used it without any bad sense, δεσμὸς ὑ. a huge bond, **[5th c. BC]** Pi. Fr. 92; οἶνον ὑπερφίαλον κελαρύζετε pour the noble wine, or pour it without stint, Ion Trag. **[5th c. BC]** 10:—this notion appears most clearly in the Adv. ὑπερφιάλως, exceedingly, excessively, **[8th c. BC]** ὑ. νεμεσᾶν Il. 13.293, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 17.481, **[8th c. BC]** 21.285; **[8th c. BC]** ἀνιάζειν Il. 18.300: but the Adv. also passed into the sense of haughtily, arrogantly, **[8th c. BC]** Od. 1.227, **[8th c. BC]** 4.663, etc. (The old deriv. from ὑπὲρ φιάλην, running over (cf. Ion l.c.), is improbable, but modern explanations are unconvincing.) [^lsj]
