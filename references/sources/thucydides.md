# Thucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War

The primary, near-contemporary historical account of the Peloponnesian
War (431-404 BC) by an Athenian who lived through it and, by his own
account, sought to interview participants on both sides. Widely regarded
by modern historians as the most methodologically careful of the
surviving ancient historians — he explicitly distinguishes eyewitness
testimony from hearsay in his own preface (I.22).

**Status: integrated.** Cited directly (via `extra_sources`, not merely
paraphrased through Athenaze) in [`culture/athenian-farmer-class-system.md`](../../culture/athenian-farmer-class-system.md)
for the II.14 passage on rural Athenians' reluctance to relocate inside
the city walls at the war's outbreak.

**Authority:** `historical: 5` — as high as the scale goes for an ancient
source: primary, contemporary, and self-consciously methodical about
distinguishing evidence quality. Not infallible (his speeches are
acknowledged rhetorical reconstructions, not transcripts) — but that
caveat governs how any one passage gets used, not a downward adjustment
of the source's overall historical standing.
