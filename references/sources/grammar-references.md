# Grammar references and textbooks

A survey of notable Ancient Greek grammars/textbooks, spanning the
Western analytical tradition and the Russian academic tradition —
candidate citation sources for this KB's "Grammatical Rule" concept
type, distinct from the lexical/corpus sources above.

**Western analytical grammars:**
- Kühner (Raphael Kühner), *Ausführliche Grammatik der griechischen
  Sprache* — the classic reference, especially in the Kühner-Gerth
  revision; deep analytical treatment of syntax and the history of
  forms.
- Gildersleeve (Basil Lanneau Gildersleeve), *Syntax of Classical Greek
  from Homer to Demosthenes* — analytical grammar built from real
  syntactic contexts rather than rule-memorization, explaining *why*
  authors chose a given construction.

**Russian academic textbooks:**
- Sobolevsky (Сергей Иванович Соболевский), *Древнегреческий язык*
  (1st ed. 1948) — the standard full course for classical philology in
  Russian: phonetics, morphology, syntax, exercises, adapted texts,
  with regular comparison to Latin. Available at
  `https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Spravochniki/drevnegrecheskij-jazyk/`.
- Kozarzhevsky (Андрей Чеславович Козаржевский), *Учебник
  древнегреческого языка* — written for non-philologists (historians,
  philosophers); 38 lessons combining syntax and morphology for a
  faster start. Available at
  `https://azbyka.ru/otechnik/Spravochniki/uchebnik-drevnegrecheskogo-jazyka-kozarzhevskij/`.

**Reading-based courses:**
- Wolf (Herwig Wolf, Hans-Joachim Glücklich, et al.), *Propyläen: Ein
  Lehrgang des Griechischen* — German reading-based course: near-
  original texts from the first chapters, grammar drawn from them
  rather than taught up front. An earlier Friedrich Wolf chrestomathy
  in the same tradition underlies Yuri Shichalin's Russian *Древнегреческий
  язык. Начальный курс* (Greco-Latin Cabinet), widely used in Russian
  seminaries and universities.

**Historical/comparative grammar:**
- Chantraine (Pierre Chantraine), *Morphologie historique du grec* —
  translated into Russian by Яков Маркович Боровский (1953), with his
  own substantial preface and notes cross-referencing Sobolevsky's
  textbook. Explains *why* forms took their attested shape via
  comparative Indo-European reconstruction (Latin, Sanskrit, Old Church
  Slavonic) — an advanced-level companion once basic paradigms
  (Kozarzhevsky/Sobolevsky) are already known. Russian edition
  available at
  `https://www.gumer.info/bibliotek_Buks/Linguist/shantr/index.php`.

**Status: not integrated.** These are print/scanned reference works,
not structured data — documented here as citation sources for
grammatical-rule content, not as pipeline inputs.
