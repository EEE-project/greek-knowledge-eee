# Pseudo-Xenophon, Constitution of the Athenians

A short 5th-century-BC political pamphlet on Athenian democracy,
traditionally transmitted among Xenophon's works but not actually by him
(hence "Pseudo-Xenophon," also called "the Old Oligarch") — written from
an explicitly anti-democratic aristocratic viewpoint.

**Status: integrated.** Cited directly (via `extra_sources`) in
`culture/slavery-in-ancient-athens.md` for its I.10 remark on slaves and
metics, and the practical impossibility of telling them apart from
citizens by dress alone.

**Authority:** `historical: {min: 2, max: 4}` — a genuine range, not a
single number, because its reliability depends on what claim it's cited
to support. As a witness to what an aristocratic 5th-century Athenian
actually believed and complained about, it's a valuable, hard-to-replace
primary source (toward 4). As a neutral report of objective social
conditions, it is explicitly polemical and one-sided (toward 2) — unlike
Thucydides, it makes no claim to disinterested method. Real historians
read it skeptically for exactly this reason; collapsing it to a single
point score would overstate a certainty this source doesn't have.
