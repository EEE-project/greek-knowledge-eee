# Irby, "Squaring the Circle and Saving the Phenomena: Reading Science in the Greek Language Classroom" (2017)

Georgia Irby (College of William & Mary), published in *Teaching
Classical Languages* 8.2 (2017): 85–153
(`https://tcl.camws.org/sites/default/files/TCL%208.2%20Irby.pdf`). Not
a sequential course like
[[`references/sources/greek-for-euclid.md`](greek-for-euclid.md)](greek-for-euclid.md), but a
professionally curated, annotated anthology of dozens of short Ancient
Greek passages — full Greek text, grammar/syntax tags, and per-word
vocabulary notes for each, graded-reader style — drawn from primary
sources across multiple scientific disciplines. Confirmed sections:
III. Matter theory, IV. Arithmetic and Geometry (includes an excerpt
from Euclid's own *Elements*, Definitions 1–4), V. Astronomy,
VI. Meteorology, VII. Geography and Cartography, VIII. The Origin of
Life, IX. Botany, X. Zoology (sections I–II, and anything beyond X,
not checked). Authors excerpted include Homer, Aristotle, Plato,
Theophrastus, Aristophanes, Herodotus, Strabo, and the Presocratics.

**Status: not integrated.** Open-access PDF, licensed **CC BY 3.0**
(*Teaching Classical Languages* is an open-access CAMWS journal) — the
most openly-licensed of this batch's pedagogical finds. A genuinely
reusable per-passage citation source for Grammatical Rule or vocabulary
content touching ancient science, though each entry is a short excerpt
rather than a full text. Notably has **no Medicine/Hippocrates
section**, despite discussing Galen and Hippocratic method at length in
its introduction — a real gap if this KB ever wants a Greek-medicine
angle; none of this batch's sources cover Hippocrates specifically. See
[[`references/sources/greek-for-euclid.md`](greek-for-euclid.md)](greek-for-euclid.md) for the
one narrow-domain course this list does have, on mathematical Greek.
