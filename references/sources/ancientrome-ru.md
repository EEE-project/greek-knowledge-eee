# ancientrome.ru

A Russian-language classical-antiquity portal (`https://ancientrome.ru/`).
Despite its Rome-centric branding, it indexes substantial Greek material:

- `antlitr/` (`https://ancientrome.ru/antlitr/index.htm`) — a catalog of
  classical authors and works covering both Greek (Homer, Herodotus,
  Plato, Aristotle, Aeschylus, Aristophanes, Pausanias, Plutarch, Strabo,
  and more) and Roman authors, with links to original-language texts,
  Russian translations, and external databases (Perseus, Loeb Classical
  Library, LacusCurtius).
- `dictio/` (`https://ancientrome.ru/dictio/index.htm`) — a catalog of
  reference dictionaries, including the Dvoretsky Ancient Greek–Russian
  dictionary (Древнегреческо-русский словарь) and Autenrieth's *A
  Homeric Dictionary for Schools and Colleges* — both directly relevant
  given this KB's existing Homeric/Odyssey vocabulary content and
  Russian-language labels.

**Status: a finding aid, and the second host of one translation.** The page
`antlitr/homer/index.htm` hosts V. V. Veresaev's Russian translation of the Odyssey
(24 songs, `antlitr/t.htm?a=13440300NN`) and of the Homeric Hymns on the site itself,
with the edition statement `Moscow, Гос. изд-во худож. лит-ры, 1953`. Each song page is a bilingual
publication: it shows the Greek text (named on the page as Homer, The Odyssey with an English
translation by A. T. Murray, London, Heinemann, 1919) beside the Russian, and a button hides the
Greek. The Veresaev section of `texts/odyssey/translations_ru.md` (Book I and Book IX) equals songs 1
and 9 on this site word for word, as it equals az.lib.ru. The index page offers no other Odyssey
translation: Murray's English text is only linked as scans on archive.org, and the Greek texts
are linked editions (Dindorf, Monro, von der Mühll). Otherwise the site is mostly a finding aid
rather than a bulk data source. Both sections primarily catalog and link to external
resources rather than hosting structured, machine-readable entries
directly (the site does claim ~6,783 articles across its own on-site
dictionaries, but that wasn't verified beyond the index page). License/
copyright status is not stated on the pages checked. Worth revisiting
per-resource — the Dvoretsky dictionary in particular — if a genuinely
structured, licensable copy can be located.
