## Ioannis Kapodistrias, the first Governor of Greece

Before entering Greek politics, **Ioannis Kapodistrias** (1776-1831) had an entire diplomatic career at the Russian imperial court: he served **Tsar Alexander I** as a senior official and, from 1816, as joint Foreign Minister of the Russian Empire — a career built on negotiation and international coordination rather than on Greek affairs specifically.[^wiki-kapodistrias]

In 1827, with the Greek War of Independence (begun 1821) still unresolved, the Third National Assembly at Troezen elected Kapodistrias as **Kyvernitis** (Governor) of the nascent Greek state — its first head of state. He arrived in 1828 to a country devastated by war: widespread poverty, famine, and a near-total absence of functioning institutions, courts, or schools. His short government (1828-1831) focused on building the basic apparatus of a state from nothing — administration, justice, education, and a national currency (the phoenix) — while balancing the competing interests of the Great Powers (Russia, Britain, France) who had underwritten Greek independence.[^wiki-kapodistrias]

**Nafplio**, not Athens, served as his seat of government and the first capital of independent Greece; Athens only became the capital later, under King Otto, in 1834. Kapodistrias's centralizing style made him powerful local enemies, particularly among the Maniot clan leaders of the Peloponnese. He was assassinated in Nafplio on 9 October 1831 by members of the Mavromichalis family, cutting his state-building work short less than four years after it began.[^wiki-kapodistrias]
