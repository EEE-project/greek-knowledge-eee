## The historical setting of Athenaze's narrative

Athenaze's fictional story of Dikaiopolis and his family is set against a precise historical backdrop: from autumn 433 to spring 431 BC, at the height of Athenian democracy under **Pericles**. Athens dominates the sea and holds a large empire, but its power provokes fear and envy in Sparta and its Peloponnesian League allies — above all Corinth. By spring 431, Athens and the Peloponnesian League are already at war — a war that will end, twenty-seven years later, in Athens's defeat.[^athenaze-ch1]

The story's main plot resolves by Chapters 18-20; the second book's opening draws the family into the Athens-Corinth conflict that actually triggered the Peloponnesian War, and Chapters 21-23 (set at the war's outbreak) are based directly on Thucydides's own history of the conflict.[^athenaze-ch1]
